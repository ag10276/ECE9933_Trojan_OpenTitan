Explanation:
The Trojan employs a 10-bit counter to silently count the number of encryption operations; when 862 encryptions are reached, a disable flag is triggered. Once active, the Trojan permanently halts AES operation by forcing 'ready' low and 'result_valid' low, denying all further outputs.

Trigger:
The Trojan is triggered internally after exactly 862 AES encryptions (when encdec=1) are performed, tracked by a counter that increments only on valid encryption cycles. Once this threshold is reached, the disable flag is permanently set, regardless of future resets.

Payload:
The payload indefinitely disables the AES core by forcing the FSM into a stalled condition, never asserting 'ready' or 'result_valid', thus denying all AES encryption and decryption operations from this point onward, effectively causing a Denial of Service.
